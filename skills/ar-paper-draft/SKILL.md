# Academic Paper Drafting (Single Section-by-Section Prose)

## Overview

This skill transforms structured academic paper blueprints (such as those generated by `ar-paper-outline`) into publication-grade manuscript prose. It enforces **iterative, single section-by-section drafting**: writing exactly **one modular file at a time** (e.g., `01_introduction.md` or a specific sub-section file). It adheres strictly to paragraph objectives, narrative sequences, word-count allocations, and rigorous academic writing standards.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **No Citation Numbering in This Step**
> In Step 1, prose is drafted **without numeric reference tags** (do NOT insert `[1]`, `[[1]]`, or links like `[[1]](06_references.md#ref1)`). Citation curation (`references.txt`), reference list compilation (`06_references.md`), and in-text citation numbering are executed in downstream steps (Steps 2, 3, and 4). Every sentence must be crafted as a crisp, verifiable scholarly assertion ready for sentence-level citation mapping.

## When to Activate

- User asks to write, draft, or generate a specific section or sub-section of an academic paper based on an outline (e.g., `01_introduction.md`, `02_related-works.md`, `03_materials-and-methods_a-dataset.md`, etc.).
- User invokes trigger phrases: `draft paper`, `buat draft paper`, `tulis naskah`, `draft section`, `write introduction`, `write methodology`, `write discussion`, `draft manuscript`, `paper draft`.
- User provides a paragraph-by-paragraph outline and asks to draft the next section.

## When NOT to Activate

- Drafting all sections simultaneously in a single command (always draft one section at a time).
- Creating, planning, or structuring paper outlines from scratch (use `ar-paper-outline`).
- Searching, curating, or mapping citations to bibliographic records (use citation curation skills / Step 2).
- Compiling reference lists (`06_references.md`) or assigning in-text citation numbering (use Step 3 & 4 skills).
- Synthesizing abstracts or keywords in isolation (use abstract generation skills / Step 5).
- Performing peer-review critique or reviewer scoring.
- Non-academic writing (blogs, documentation, marketing copy).

## Scope

- **In scope:** Drafting one modular Markdown section or sub-section file per request, Swales CARS Introduction rhetoric, Claim-Evidence-Reasoning (CER) results interpretation, sentence-level claim precision, relative section roadmap hyperlinking, word count tracking, and anti-slop lexical control.
- **Out of scope:** Generating the entire paper at once, citation numbering (`[1]`, `[[1]]`), reference list generation, running LaTeX compilation pipelines, or automated journal submission.

## Mandatory Step 0: Intake & Scope Verification

Before writing any prose, verify the prerequisite parameters:

1. **Target Section Identification**: Confirm which **single section or sub-section file** is being drafted (e.g., `01_introduction.md` or `03_materials-and-methods_b-[technique].md`). **Never attempt to draft the whole paper at once.**
2. **Paragraph Blueprint Ingestion**: Read the approved paragraph blueprint for that specific section (objectives, narrative points, target word count, and assigned empirical claims).
3. **Strict No-Numbering Policy**: Verify that no numeric citation markers will be emitted in the text. Focus on clear, well-supported scholarly statements.
4. **Empirical Materials & Figures**: Confirm that the necessary experimental numbers, tables, or algorithmic steps for this section are available. Never fabricate data.
5. **Acronym Registry Inspection**: Read `paper/acronyms.txt` (if present) to identify all previously introduced abbreviations, ensuring already-registered terms are never re-expanded in this section.

## Modular File Architecture

Each section or sub-section is drafted into its own individual Markdown file under the target directory (e.g., `paper/`):

```text
paper/
├── 01_introduction.md                                      # Context, CARS niche, contributions, roadmap
├── 02_related-works.md                                     # Thematic synthesis of prior art
├── 03_materials-and-methods_0-overview.md                  # System pipeline & architectural overview
├── 03_materials-and-methods_a-dataset.md                   # Dataset characteristics & preprocessing
├── 03_materials-and-methods_b-[method-name].md             # Mathematical models & feature extraction
├── 03_materials-and-methods_g-classification-pipeline.md   # Training setup & leakage-guard protocols
├── 03_materials-and-methods_h-evaluation-metrics.md        # Metrics & cross-validation definitions
├── 04_results-and-discussion_a-global-performance.md       # Primary benchmark comparisons
├── 04_results-and-discussion_b-feature-ablation-study.md   # Component ablation experiments
├── 04_results-and-discussion_c-subgroup-performance.md     # Fine-grained subgroup analysis
├── 04_results-and-discussion_d-error-pattern-assessment.md # Error diagnostics & failure cases
├── 04_results-and-discussion_e-comparison-with-prior-art.md# Juxtaposition with published literature
├── 05_conclusion.md                                        # Synthesis, limitations, future directions
└── acronyms.txt                                            # Centralized acronym & abbreviation registry
```

## Core Drafting Protocols

### 1. Single-Section Discipline
Draft exactly **one modular file at a time**. Focus cognitive attention entirely on completing that section with maximum scholarly rigor, satisfying all paragraph blueprint specifications before moving to user review.

### 2. Sentence-Level Claim Formulation (Pre-Citation Grounding)
Construct each sentence as a discrete, verifiable assertion:
- Write substantive scientific claims directly in the text without attaching bracketed numbers (`[1]`, `[[1]]`).
- Ensure each sentence carrying an external empirical or theoretical claim is clearly bounded so that in Step 2, it can be mapped to its corresponding `.bib` or `.ris` entry in `references.txt`.
- If an empirical fact lacks evidence in the outline, mark it as `[GAP: source needed]` rather than inventing a reference or fabricating a citation number.

### 3. Acronym & Abbreviation Registry Protocol (`acronyms.txt`)
All technical abbreviations across modular section files are governed by a centralized registry (`paper/acronyms.txt`):
- **Pre-Draft Check**: Consult `paper/acronyms.txt` before drafting to identify abbreviations introduced in earlier files.
- **First-Mention Full Form**: The very first time an acronym appears anywhere in the manuscript, write its full name followed by the abbreviation in parentheses: e.g., `Vision Transformer (ViT)`, `Support Vector Machine (SVM)`, `Principal Component Analysis (PCA)`.
- **Subsequent Acronym-Only**: In all subsequent mentions across all files, use **only the abbreviation** (`ViT`, `SVM`, `PCA`). **Never re-expand an acronym that has already been registered.**
- **Post-Draft Registration**: If the drafted section introduces new technical abbreviations, append them immediately to `paper/acronyms.txt` logging: number, acronym, full form, and first introduction location (e.g., `paper/00_abstract.md (Abstract)` or `paper/01_introduction.md (P3)`).

### 4. Rhetorical Section Standards
- **Introduction (`01_` )**: Follow Swales' CARS moves:
  * Move 1: Establish territory (centrality claim, foundational literature synthesis).
  * Move 2: Establish niche (explicit, scoped research gap; avoid unverifiable blanket negatives).
  * Move 3: Occupy niche (concise purpose statement, numbered list of explicit scientific contributions, and a roadmap paragraph linking to subsequent sections via Markdown links, e.g., `Section [II](02_related-works.md)...`).
- **Related Works (`02_` )**: Synthesize by theme or problem domain rather than chronological author-lists. Compare prior approaches directly against the proposed work.
- **Methods (`03_` )**: Provide exhaustive procedural detail. State variable definitions for every mathematical symbol. Document data-leakage safeguards.
- **Results & Discussion (`04_` )**: Apply the CER framework (Claim $\rightarrow$ Evidence $\rightarrow$ Reasoning $\rightarrow$ Qualification). Always cite exact figures/tables before interpreting mechanisms.
- **Conclusion (`05_` )**: Deliver the 4 pillars: restatement of core achievement, synthesis of principal findings, explicit methodological limitations, and actionable future research directions.

### 5. Writing Quality & Anti-Slop Guardrails
- **Banned AI Cliches**: Eliminate buzzwords (`delve`, `tapestry`, `pivotal`, `crucial`, `showcase`, `testament`, `multifaceted`, `plethora`, `groundbreaking`). Use precise academic alternatives (`investigate`, `interplay`, `critical`, `demonstrate`, `evidence`, `complex`).
- **Eliminate Throat-Clearing**: Remove filler openers (*"It is important to note that..."*, *"In the realm of..."*, *"It goes without saying that..."*).
- **Punctuation Limits**: Em dashes ($\le 2$ per paper); semicolons ($\le 2$ per 1,000 words).
- **Sentence Burstiness**: Alternate short impactful sentences (8–14 words) with complex explanatory sentences (25–38 words). Never write 4+ consecutive sentences of similar length.
- **Hedging Discipline**: Hedge interpretations and hypotheses (*"suggests"*, *"indicates"*, *"may reflect"*), but never hedge empirical measurements (*"achieved 94.2%"*) or established methodology (*"was evaluated using"*).

## Do and Don't Guidelines

| Do | Don't |
|:---|:---|
| Draft exactly one section or sub-section file per turn | Attempt to generate the whole paper or multiple sections at once |
| Follow the approved paragraph blueprint and word budgets | Drift into unstructured stream-of-consciousness writing |
| Write clean, discrete claims ready for Step 2 citation mapping | Insert citation numbers (`[1]`, `[[1]]`) prematurely |
| Maintain relative Markdown hyperlinks across sections | Leave section roadmap references unlinked |
| Formulate scoped, defensible research gaps | Use universal negatives (*"No study has ever examined..."*) |
| Acknowledge concrete methodological limitations in the conclusion | Claim flawless performance or zero bias |
| Vary sentence lengths to create natural academic rhythm | Produce metronomic sentences with uniform lengths |
| Define all mathematical variables immediately around equations | Introduce floating mathematical symbols without definitions |
| Consult `paper/acronyms.txt` before drafting and register all new acronyms | Re-expand acronyms already in `acronyms.txt` or introduce unexpanded abbreviations |
