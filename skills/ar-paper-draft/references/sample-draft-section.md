# I. INTRODUCTION

Automated analysis of high-dimensional visual biometric data plays an indispensable role across modern intelligent systems, including digital forensics, biometric access verification, and human-computer interaction. Despite notable strides in raw feature extraction, recent empirical evaluations demonstrate that state-of-the-art vision models frequently exhibit substantial performance disparities across distinct demographic subgroups. These systematic performance gaps often stem from long-tail distribution skews in benchmark training corpora, compounded by architectural limitations in capturing subtle morphological features across intersectional identities. Consequently, developing unified feature representation strategies that mitigate subgroup bias while preserving discriminative fidelity remains a pressing challenge in applied computer vision.

Traditional facial attribute classification pipelines typically treat demographic attributes as isolated, independent prediction targets, overlooking the intrinsic correlations between intersectional facets such as race, gender, and biological age. When attributes are modeled independently, downstream classifiers fail to resolve feature interactions unique to compound intersectional cohorts, leading to masked error rates when evaluated exclusively on aggregate population metrics. Controlled empirical evaluations across balanced cohorts provide an objective methodology for exposing these hidden subgroup variances. Furthermore, multi-attribute visual modeling requires latent representations that decouple task-relevant biometric signals from transient environmental variations, such as lighting fluctuations and pose alterations.

Methodological advances in facial feature modeling have transitioned from handcrafted morphological descriptors to deep representation learning frameworks, notably Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and Vision Transformers (ViTs). Convolutional approaches initially demonstrated efficacy in localized facial landmark extraction and multi-task demographic regression. More recently, Vision Transformers have emerged as a dominant paradigm due to their Multi-Head Self-Attention (MHSA) mechanisms, which capture global patch dependencies across the entire visual receptive field. Parallel multi-axis attention designs and hybrid generative feature synthesizers have further advanced latent representation quality. Nevertheless, existing transformer architectures primarily rely on single-domain visual embeddings, leaving the structured fusion of affective, morphological, and age-invariant latent domains largely unexplored.

Despite the rapid evolution of transformer-based architectures, three foundational research gaps persist. First, the majority of contemporary frameworks rely on unimodal feature spaces, lacking systematic mechanisms to fuse complementary affective and morphological latent representations for intersectional analysis. Second, facial recognition architectures frequently display heightened sensitivity to age-related facial degradation because standard feature extractors omit explicit biological aging representations. Third, systematic comparative benchmarks evaluating downstream classifier behavior, feature normalization techniques, and dimensionality reduction protocols across transformer embeddings remain scarce in the literature. Resolving these representation bottlenecks necessitates a multi-domain fusion strategy paired with a rigorously tuned classification pipeline.

To resolve these empirical and architectural gaps, this study presents a Tri-Domain Feature Fusion framework that integrates distinct task-specific latent representations extracted from frozen Vision Transformer backbones. Specifically, the framework fuses biometric structural features, affective facial expressions, and biological aging markers into a unified high-dimensional latent vector. Feature extraction is conducted offline, eliminating the computational overhead of end-to-end backbone retraining. The resulting multi-domain representations are systematically benchmarked across linear, probabilistic, ensemble, and kernel-based downstream classifiers. Crucially, all feature scaling transformations and dimensionality reduction matrices are fitted strictly on training partitions within a Stratified Cross-Validation scheme to eliminate data leakage.

The primary scientific contributions of this investigation are summarized as follows:
1. **Tri-Domain Latent Fusion Architecture**: We develop a unified feature fusion framework that combines structural biometric, affective expression, and age-associated latent embeddings from pre-trained Vision Transformers into a composite feature space for intersectional classification.
2. **Comprehensive Empirical Benchmark**: We conduct systematic evaluations across single-domain, dual-domain, and tri-domain configurations using four classical machine learning classifiers optimized via hyperparameter grid searches.
3. **Rigorous Intersectional Subgroup Evaluation**: We provide a fine-grained subgroup diagnostic across multiple intersectional cohorts, demonstrating that multi-domain fusion significantly reduces inter-group performance disparities compared to single-domain baselines.
4. **Leakage-Guarded Evaluation Protocol**: We implement an open, reproducible experimental pipeline that strictly separates representation learning from downstream optimization, ensuring valid empirical comparisons.

The remainder of this article is organized as follows: Section [II](02_related-works.md) reviews prior literature on demographic classification and transformer backbones. Section [III](03_materials-and-methods_0-overview.md) delineates the dataset specifications, feature extraction pipeline, and downstream classifier optimization protocols. Section [IV](04_results-and-discussion_a-global-performance.md) analyzes experimental benchmark findings, feature ablation results, and intersectional subgroup disparities. Finally, Section [V](05_conclusion.md) summarizes core findings, delineates methodological limitations, and outlines future research trajectories.
